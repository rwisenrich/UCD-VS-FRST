## Central proposition

Reality is modeled as one finite, relationally constrained quantum system. Space, time, particles, fields, geometry, classical records and probability are treated as organizations/observables of that system rather than separate primitive substances.

The hierarchy is

`mathematical possibility -> physical admissibility -> actual universal state -> observable reality`.

## Twelve frozen axioms

1. **Finite actuality:** `0 < |V| = N < infinity`; each primitive identity has a finite-dimensional local state space.
2. **Relational primacy:** primitive labels have no physical meaning apart from invariant relations.
3. **Completed carrier:** once the carrier exists, later evolution does not manufacture additional primitive identities.
4. **Finite relational structure:** a finite relational complex `K=(V,E,F,...)` carries possible pair/higher relations.
5. **Constraint selection:** physically admissible states satisfy `C_alpha |Psi> = 0`.
6. **Complex state principle:** coherent alternatives are represented in a complex Hilbert space.
7. **Closed universal evolution:** the complete system evolves within its physical Hilbert space without a fundamental external environment/collapse rule.
8. **Locality of law:** fundamental interactions have bounded relational support or sufficient relational decay.
9. **Internal time:** physical time is reconstructed from subsystem change/correlation, not an external primitive clock.
10. **Relational geometry:** spatial geometry is reconstructed from relational/link state.
11. **Probability consistency:** physical probabilities are normalized, additive on mutually exclusive alternatives and representation/context consistent.
12. **Observable closure:** physically meaningful quantities belong to the invariant physical observable algebra.

## Master state architecture

For finite local factors,

`H_raw = tensor_{x in K} H_x`, with `dim H_x < infinity`.

The physical subspace is

`H_phys = intersection_alpha ker(C_alpha) = P_phys H_raw`,

where `P_phys` is the orthogonal projector onto the intersection. The physical observable algebra is the constrained invariant operator algebra, schematically

`A_phys = { O = P_phys O P_phys : U_g O U_g^dagger = O }`.

A relationally local physical Hamiltonian has the form

`H = P_phys (sum_{X subset K} h_X) P_phys`, with `[H,P_phys]=0`.

The state evolves as

`rho(s) = exp(-isH) rho_0 exp(isH)`.

Observable probabilities are represented as

`p(o|rho) = Tr(rho P_o)`

once the quantum-probability premises are satisfied.

## Relational geometry

For edge-length observables `L_e`,

`ell_e(rho)=Tr(rho L_e)`

and

`d_rho(i,j)=min_{gamma:i->j} sum_{e in gamma} ell_e(rho)`.

Thus geometry can change while the primitive carrier remains fixed. Cosmic expansion is not identified with creation of new primitive identities.

## Frozen boundary

FRST v1.0 does **not** derive the numerical model of our universe. Its unresolved specification data include the carrier size/complex, local dimensions, exact physical constraints, exact Hamiltonian/couplings and actual initial/boundary state. The deepest unresolved question is why one particular triple `(P_phys,H,rho_0)` is selected rather than another.

FRST v1.0 assumes finite actuality. UCD must not import that assumption as a theorem; the comparison project exists in part to test whether UCD can derive a corresponding result upstream.
